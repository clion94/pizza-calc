⚠ Over-fermentation

If dough comes out of the fridge airy and sticky, too much activity happened before refrigerating. Limit bulk rest to 45–60 minutes in a warm kitchen. Dough should look nearly identical to when you finished mixing — smooth, not puffy.

⚠ Tearing when stretching

Cold gluten snaps — warmed gluten stretches. After pulling from the fridge, rest 90–120 minutes at room temperature before attempting to stretch. If it springs back, cover and wait another 10–15 minutes.

⚠ Sticking to hands

Flour hands with bread flour before handling cold dough — never wet hands. Dust the bench lightly with semolina for best results.

Ball Shaping
01 Place dough on a bare, unfloured surface. Friction against the counter is what creates surface tension — flour here defeats the purpose.
02 Cup hand over dough and drag toward you with light downward pressure. The counter grips the bottom skin and pulls it tight. Rotate and repeat 8–10 times.
03 Surface should feel taut like a drum. Pinch the seam firmly underneath to seal.
04 Place seam-down in a lightly oiled container just barely larger than the ball. Oversized containers cause dough to spread flat and go slack during fermentation.

Baking Sheet Protocol
Invert and preheat 45 minutes
Flip the baking sheet upside down and place on the bottom rack. Set oven to 550°F or maximum temperature. Allow the full 45 minutes — a fully saturated pan is the difference between a crispy undercarriage and a pale, soft one.
Bottom rack → top rack
Bake 4–5 minutes on the bottom rack to set and crisp the base, then transfer to the top rack until cheese is fully bubbled and edges are lightly charred.
Build on semolina
Dust the cutting board or peel with semolina flour before building the pizza. It acts like ball bearings. Shake the board gently before launching to confirm the dough slides freely.
Go light on sauce
Use 3–4 tablespoons of sauce maximum. Strain crushed tomatoes through a fine sieve before spreading — excess moisture prevents the crust from crisping on a baking sheet.
